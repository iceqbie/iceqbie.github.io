Research

Space plasma physics, Department of Earth and Planetary Science, The University of Tokyo

Core question

I work on how collisionless turbulence divides its energy between ions and electrons — and on whether simulations conserve well enough for that number to mean anything, at a cost anyone can afford.

Current work

Machine-learning closures

My current research: can data-driven closure models reproduce kinetic plasma behavior in affordable fluid simulations? A heat-flux closure and a heating prescription are the same kind of object.

Questions

  1. Q1

    The species split

    Turbulence delivers energy at large scales; it is absorbed at small ones. What sets the ion-to-electron heating ratio as a function of β and Tᵢ/Tₑ? The dependence is steep enough to change what an accretion flow looks like.

  2. Q2

    Heat or a nonthermal tail

    The same energy can thermalise or end up in a power-law tail of fast particles. How much goes where, and what sets the slope? The answer is a fitted distribution function with an honest error bar.

  3. Q3

    Which mechanism dissipates

    Landau damping, stochastic heating, reconnection in the thin sheets turbulence makes, or the firehose and mirror instabilities — turbulence regulating itself. Each predicts a different split, and which wins depends on β and scale.

  4. Q4

    Physics, or the code?

    Standard explicit PIC heats the plasma numerically, and every published heating fraction comes from such runs. Nobody has systematically asked how much of it survives a change of scheme.

  5. Q5

    What does it cost?

    An energy-conserving scheme needs a global implicit solve where explicit PIC needs none, and GPUs favour explicit PIC. Whether larger timesteps pay for the solve cannot be answered from theory — it has to be measured.

  6. Deeper

    How does a reversible system become irreversible?

    The Vlasov equation is time-reversible and conserves fine-grained entropy, yet plasmas heat and heating cannot be undone. A grid coarse-grains whether asked to or not — so is the irreversibility nature's, or the grid's?
